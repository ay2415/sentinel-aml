# Typology Guide - Structuring and Cash Placement

## Description
Structuring (also called smurfing) is the deliberate splitting of cash deposits or withdrawals into amounts below reporting or monitoring thresholds to avoid scrutiny. In the EU, cash transactions of EUR 10,000 or more attract heightened due diligence, so structured deposits frequently cluster in the EUR 7,500 to EUR 9,999 range.

## Red flag indicators
- Multiple cash deposits just below EUR 10,000 over a short period, often across different branches or ATMs.
- Cash deposits inconsistent with the customer's occupation, declared income or expected turnover.
- Cash placement followed shortly afterwards by international wires or transfers to unrelated third parties.
- Customer reluctance to explain the source of cash or provide supporting evidence.
- Use of many branches in different locations within days.

## Distinguishing legitimate cash-intensive businesses
Retailers, hospitality businesses and takeaways lodge takings regularly. Legitimate patterns usually show: deposits at a consistent home branch; amounts that follow trading cycles such as weekend peaks; supplier payments, payroll and tax payments funded from the takings; and total cash volume consistent with the declared turnover and the size of the premises. An occasional weekly lodgement just under EUR 10,000 from an established cash business with consistent history is weak evidence on its own.

## Investigation steps
1. Compare cash volumes against the declared expected turnover and prior months.
2. Check branch dispersion and timing of deposits.
3. Trace where the cash-funded balance goes next, especially international wires.
4. Review prior alerts and prior customer contact.
5. Consider requesting source-of-funds evidence where suspicion is not yet formed.

## Escalation criteria
Escalate to the MLRO for STR consideration where repeated threshold-avoidance deposits have no plausible business explanation, especially when combined with onward transfers to third parties or high-risk jurisdictions.
