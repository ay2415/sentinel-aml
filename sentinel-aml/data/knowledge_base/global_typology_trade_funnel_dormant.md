# Typology Guide - Funnel Accounts, Trade Wires and Dormant Account Reactivation

## Funnel accounts
A funnel account receives multiple cash deposits, often in different locations, and the accumulated funds are moved out in large transfers, commonly international wires. Red flags include: deposits at many different branches in a short period; deposit volumes far above the business's historic turnover; large outgoing wires to trading hubs shortly after cash accumulation; and wire references describing goods purchases without matching supplier history.

## Trade-based wires to high-risk jurisdictions
Trade-based money laundering disguises value transfer as commercial trade. Indicators include: funds received from one jurisdiction and forwarded within days to a high-risk third country; round or repeated amounts; references such as "machinery parts", "consulting" or "goods" from an account whose profile does not include import or export activity; and inability to provide invoices, bills of lading or contracts.

## Dormant account reactivation
Accounts inactive for long periods can be sold or taken over for laundering. Red flags include: a long period of no activity followed by sudden high-value inflows; immediate onward transfers, cash withdrawals or crypto purchases; changes to contact details shortly before reactivation; and activity inconsistent with the historical profile.

## Legitimate explanations
Dormant accounts are sometimes reactivated when customers return from abroad, receive inheritance or restart a business. These cases usually include documentary evidence, a coherent explanation and ordinary spending patterns after reactivation.

## Escalation criteria
Escalate where the pattern shows accumulation and rapid onward movement without an evidenced commercial or personal rationale, particularly when combined with high-risk jurisdictions or crypto-asset exits.
