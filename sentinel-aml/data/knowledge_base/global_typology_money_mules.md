# Typology Guide - Money Mule Accounts and Rapid Pass-Through

## Description
A money mule receives criminal proceeds, often from fraud victims, into their own account and quickly forwards them, frequently to crypto-asset exchanges or overseas accounts. Mules are commonly recruited through social media with offers of easy money and are often young adults or students. Complicit and unwitting mules both present risk; the account behaviour is what monitoring can observe.

## Red flag indicators
- Recently opened account with a sudden increase in incoming payments from many unrelated senders.
- Incoming funds forwarded out within hours or a day, leaving a near-zero balance (pass-through behaviour).
- Outgoing transfers to crypto-asset service providers or international beneficiaries with no prior history.
- Vague or informal payment references such as "gift", "loan back", "for the car".
- Activity at unusual hours and volumes inconsistent with a student or low-income profile.
- Victims' banks raising fraud recalls against incoming payments.

## Distinguishing legitimate activity
Students and friend groups frequently split bills, rent and travel costs through instant payments. These payments are typically small, come from a stable group of recurring counterparties, and are spent on ordinary goods rather than being forwarded in full to exchanges. Large one-off inflows followed by outflows can also be legitimate, for example property purchase completion funds moving through a solicitor's client account.

## Investigation steps
1. Count unique incoming counterparties and compare with prior months.
2. Measure the share of inflows forwarded out within 48 hours.
3. Identify the destination channels and jurisdictions of outflows.
4. Check account age and whether the profile explains the volumes.
5. Check for fraud recall messages or prior alerts.

## Escalation criteria
Sustained pass-through of funds from multiple unrelated senders to crypto-asset exchanges or overseas accounts with no plausible explanation meets the threshold for escalation. Where there is a risk of further victim losses, the MLRO should consider account restriction in addition to reporting.
