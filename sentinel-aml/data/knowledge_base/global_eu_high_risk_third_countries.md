# High-Risk Third Countries - Enhanced Due Diligence Guidance

## Scope
The European Commission identifies third-country jurisdictions with strategic deficiencies in their AML/CFT regimes. FATF publishes jurisdictions subject to a call for action and jurisdictions under increased monitoring. The reference list used by the monitoring platform must be synchronised with the current official lists; the list embedded in the demonstration system is illustrative only.

## Required enhanced measures
For business relationships or transactions involving a high-risk third country, firms must apply enhanced due diligence including: obtaining additional information on the customer and beneficial owner; obtaining information on the intended nature of the business relationship; obtaining information on the source of funds and source of wealth; obtaining information on the reasons for the intended or performed transactions; obtaining senior management approval for establishing or continuing the relationship; and conducting enhanced monitoring by increasing the number and timing of controls.

## Red flags in payment flows
Indicators that warrant escalation include: trade-related wires to high-risk jurisdictions without supporting invoices or shipping documents; funds received from one jurisdiction and forwarded to a high-risk jurisdiction within days; vague payment references such as "goods" or "consulting" with no business rationale; round-number or repeated transfers inconsistent with the customer profile; and use of personal accounts for apparent commercial trade.

## Legitimate explanations to consider
Personal remittances to family members in affected countries are common and are not by themselves suspicious. Humanitarian support, medical expenses and family maintenance payments of modest, regular amounts that are consistent with the customer's declared circumstances and income should be documented and assessed rather than reported automatically. Sanctions screening obligations apply independently of AML assessment.

## Decision guidance
A high-risk third country nexus alone is not sufficient grounds for a suspicious transaction report. The investigator must combine the jurisdiction risk with the behaviour, the customer profile, the source of funds and the plausibility of the stated purpose.
