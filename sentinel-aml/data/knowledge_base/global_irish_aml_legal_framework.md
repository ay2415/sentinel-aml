# Irish AML/CFT Legal Framework - Obligations Summary

This summary is written for investigator training. It is not legal advice and does not replace the Act or Central Bank of Ireland guidance.

## Designated persons and the risk-based approach
Credit institutions, payment institutions and electronic money institutions operating in Ireland are designated persons. They must conduct a business-wide money laundering and terrorist financing risk assessment and apply controls proportionate to the risks identified, including customer, product, channel and geographic risk factors.

## Customer due diligence
Designated persons must identify and verify customers, understand the purpose and intended nature of the business relationship, and conduct ongoing monitoring. Ongoing monitoring includes scrutinising transactions to ensure they are consistent with the firm's knowledge of the customer, the customer's business and risk profile, and where necessary the source of funds. Customer information must be kept up to date.

## Enhanced due diligence
Enhanced due diligence is required where the risk of money laundering is higher. This includes relationships with politically exposed persons, correspondent relationships, and business relationships or transactions involving high-risk third countries identified by the European Commission. Enhanced measures may include obtaining additional information on the source of funds and source of wealth, senior management approval, and increased monitoring frequency.

## Reporting suspicious transactions
Where a designated person knows, suspects or has reasonable grounds to suspect that another person has been or is engaged in money laundering or terrorist financing, it must report that suspicion to FIU Ireland and to the Revenue Commissioners. The report must be made as soon as practicable after the suspicion is formed. Reports are submitted electronically; FIU Ireland uses the goAML platform. A designated person should not proceed with a suspicious transaction before reporting unless doing so is impracticable or would alert the customer.

## Tipping off
It is an offence to disclose to the customer or any third party that a suspicious transaction report has been made or that an investigation is being contemplated, where that disclosure is likely to prejudice an investigation. Staff must not discuss alerts, STR decisions or law enforcement contact with the customer. Customer-facing explanations for account restrictions must be approved by the MLRO.

## Record keeping
Records of customer due diligence and transactions, including internal investigation files and the rationale for decisions not to report, must be retained for at least five years after the relationship ends or the transaction occurs.

## Governance
Firms appoint a Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) with sufficient seniority and independence. The MLRO evaluates internal escalations and decides whether an external report is required. Decisions and rationale must be documented.
